# Parallel tempering for mdir: what was built, what was checked, what is not guaranteed

Branch `ccr-79b8eb7e-96dy4t`, based on the tip of `ccr-ab58fc79-sd1fvq` (0ce0f04, which contains
the merged work of the other two branches). 4 October 2026.

## Summary

1. I added **parallel tempering** (replica exchange) to the sampler as a `betas` argument of
   `callMDI()`, `runMCMCChains()` and `fitMDI()`. Its cold (`beta = 1`) chain targets the posterior for
   any ladder; this is the ergodic property you asked for. It is an asymptotic property.
   **Nothing here bounds the finite-time error**, and none can for a general multimodal posterior.
2. It is supported for complete data, `G`, `MVN` and `C` views (including semi-supervised views), without
   an outlier component. Other models are refused with an error. I did not implement the tempered
   updates for missing data, `TAGM`/`TAGPM` or GP views, and I have not derived or checked them.
3. In the multimodal example I built (four clusters, three components), **PT recovers the exact
   posterior masses of the merge patterns and independent plain chains do not**, but a single PT run is
   still noisy, it costs `length(betas)` times a plain chain, and the usual PT diagnostic (round trips)
   was badly optimistic. The reason is a sharp transition along the tempering path.
4. Pooling independent chains, as in consensus clustering, did **not** converge to the posterior in that
   example: the pooled frequencies are basin-of-attraction weights. That is one dataset and one model.

## Method

`pi_beta ∝ L^beta · P`, `L` the likelihood of the data given the labels and component parameters, `P` the
rest of the model (priors; the label coupling through weights, phis and `v`). Component parameters and
labels are drawn from their tempered conditionals (conjugate: `beta` multiplies counts and sufficient
statistics); everything else is untouched. Neighbouring replicas exchange with probability
`min(1, exp((beta_i - beta_j)(l_j - l_i)))`, `l = log L`, on an even/odd schedule (`"deo"`, Syed et al. 2022; or `"seo"`).
I chose likelihood tempering because it keeps every full conditional conjugate; the cost is the
phase transition described below.

## Multimodal example (`mode_mass.R`, `pooled_plain.R`)

Four Gaussian clusters, separation 2.5, `N = 80`, `K = 3`, fixed variance scale so the marginal
likelihood is closed form; exact posterior masses of the partitions of the four groups into at most
three blocks (exact up to items leaving their group, which is negligible here).
Exact masses: 0.522 (`1|24|3`), 0.296 (`1|2|34`), 0.099 (`12|3|4`), 0.083 (`13|2|4`), others ~0.

| | plain | PT (11 replicas) |
|---|---|---|
| runs stuck in one pattern (>95% of draws) | 10 of 12 | 0 of 12 |
| TV to exact, mean over single runs (20,000 sweeps) | 0.66 (0.48 to 0.90) | 0.16 (0.06 to 0.28) |
| TV to exact, 12 runs pooled | 0.21 | 0.012; all four modal masses within |z| < 0.5 |
| 160 independent chains pooled (6,000 sweeps), equal weights | TV 0.31; pooled 0.215/0.315/0.215/0.216 and 0.034 on a pattern with exact mass ~0 | not run |

Round trips: 0 to 3 per PT run (20,000 rounds) against 0.046 per round (about 900) predicted from the
exchange rates by Syed et al. (2022, Corollary 1). The prediction assumes each replica is in local
equilibrium; it is not. For every separation of 2 or more the chain at small `beta` sits in a
one-component phase (log-likelihood about -355 to -460, depending on the separation) and at large `beta` in a
three-component phase (about -250 to -275), with a sharp change near `beta` 0.5 to 0.8 (`scan_beta.R`, separations 2
and 3, and a first look at 4). Exchanges across it are
rare unless the ladder is dense there, and the pilot-run tuner is noisy because the transition moves.
The cold chain still changed pattern through `beta` of about 0.65 to 1.

Not done: other separations, other data, other models, a comparison at equal compute for plain chains
with a better sampler (split-merge, which I did not implement).

## Guarantees and non-guarantees

* **Holds (proved on finite spaces, checked by simulation otherwise):** each exchange, each round, each
  PT iteration leaves the product of tempered targets invariant; the `beta = 1` marginal is the posterior.
* **Not proved here:** irreducibility and ergodicity of the product chain for this sampler (no
  proof; the Gibbs updates have full support for the supported densities, so I expect it, with moderate
  confidence), a rate of convergence, and any claim about a finite run.
* **Diagnostics:** `ptDiagnostics()` reports exchange rates, the barrier and round trips. Round trips
  were misleading in the example; agreement of independent runs on mode-dependent quantities is the
  check I would trust more.
* The recorded `evidence` (`Z` at the start of the sweep) and `joint_likelihood` are of the `beta = 1` replica.
  PT does not give a marginal likelihood here; I did not implement thermodynamic integration.

## Cost and implementation notes

Replicas run in sequence in one thread (`length(betas)` times the cost); the package no longer uses
RcppParallel, and I did not add replica parallelism. At `T = 1` the code path and random-number use
are unchanged. `swap_every` is available. `callMDIWritingToFile()` does not support PT.

## Corrections made along the way

I first worked from a stale checkout (`develop`) and suspected bugs in the weight update, the
Gaussian mean update and the label swap. The branches you pointed me to had already fixed or
replaced them, so I discarded that audit. My exact reference for two views initially underflowed
(0 × -Inf with gamma weights below the smallest double); fixed by sampling on the log scale and
checking normalisation. Conda's Lean ships a `leantar` (0.1.19) that cannot read the current
Mathlib cache; I substituted 0.1.20 built from source.

## Suggested next steps

1. Run the example on your data and compare independent PT runs on co-clustering probabilities.
2. Decide whether to extend tempering to missing data and outliers (derivable in closed form for
   Gaussian and categorical, but each needs its own exact check).
3. Consider a split-merge move within replicas, and an alternative path (for example tempering only
   part of the likelihood) if the transition proves limiting on real data.
